## CORRECTION (2026-08-08): the A/B test takes a title PER VARIANT

This doc previously said "YouTube Test & Compare is thumbnail-only. One title, three
thumbnails. Never 3 title+thumbnail pairs." **That is wrong** — the live A/B Testing dialog
requires a title for every variant (variant 2's title is a required field; the test cannot be
started without it). Verified against the actual Studio UI.

Consequences for how these are written:
- The **text ≠ title** rule now applies **inside each pair**, not across the set.
- Each pair should pull ONE lever with title and image agreeing, rather than a single title
  hedging across three frames.
- Magnet words are allocated **per pair**: whichever of Free / Open Source the thumbnail does
  not already say, the title carries.
- **Read the result carefully:** title and thumbnail vary together, so a winner identifies the
  winning *pair*, not whether the words or the picture did the work. To isolate that, hold the
  title constant and vary only thumbnails on a later video.

### The logo is COMPOSITED, not generated — the important finding

`gen_thumbnail.py` passes a logo PNG to Gemini as a *reference image*, so the model **redraws it
from scratch on every roll**. Across A3-v1…v4 the same file produced a white-blown sunburst, a
13-petal symmetric flower, and a dim flat salmon star — never the actual mark. A logo is a fixed
vector asset; asking a diffusion model to reproduce it is the wrong tool.

**Shipping A3 is a composite:** the A3-v3 render is the plate, and the real
`media/library/logos/claude-1024.png` is layered on afterwards with
`tools/composite_logo.py` (written for this). That gives pixel-exact petals, exact brand colour,
and a bloom whose brightness is a parameter instead of a negotiation. The paint-out is invisible —
luminance steps across all four patch boundaries measure 2-4/255. Exact command in `A3.json`.

**Reuse this for any logo/wordmark from now on.** Prompt the plate with the logo's *space* dark
and empty, then composite. Only let the model draw a logo when it must sit in perspective on a
screen (as in A2), where a flat paste would look wrong.

### A4 — same plate, Claude mark in an app-icon tile (the vibrance fix)

**Why the bare mark reads dull, measured:** Claude terracotta `#D97757` is only 60% saturation /
137 luminance, against 86%/72% for A's cyan brain and 90%/81% for hook yellow — Anthropic chose a
calm, low-neon brand colour on purpose. Worse, the starburst **fills just 22% of its own bounding
box** (a solid tile is ~95%, a filled brain silhouette ~55%), so scaling it up mostly scales up
black, and its single flat fill has none of the internal micro-contrast the circuit-brain had.

Hero-region measurements (`_A-logo-forms.jpg` shows all three):

| | mean luminance | mean saturation |
|---|---|---|
| A — cyan circuit brain | 153/255 | 82% |
| A3 — bare Claude mark | 105/255 | 64% |
| A4 — app-icon tile | 151/255 | 66% |

The tile knocks the mark out of a solid rounded square in `#FF7A38` (78% sat / 146 lum) with a
warm-cream mark — **deliberately louder than brand**, per the loud-vs-calm rule. It recovers all
the lost luminance, gains mass and a hard silhouette at browse size, and reads as *an app*, which
partly restores the object-ness lost when the brain went away.

**Still open (strategic, not cosmetic):** a brain instantly means *thinking* to any viewer; the
Claude asterisk means nothing to someone who doesn't already know Claude. The brain→logo swap
trades broad comprehension for brand association. Worth being deliberate about before this takes
a test slot — a hybrid (mark over a brain silhouette) would recover both, at the cost of the
two-object simplicity.
